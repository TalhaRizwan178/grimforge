# Dead Men Don't Tip

**Genre:** Crime / Comedy
**Custom Genre:** Chaotic Noir
**Tone:** Witty / Dark Humour

## Plot

Ashford Finch is the worst detective in the city of Breck — and he knows it. He has solved exactly one case in six years, and that was because the criminal tripped over Finch's untied shoelace while fleeing the scene. His agency is three months behind on rent, his only client is a woman who keeps hiring him to find her cat (the same cat, seven times), and his car has not started since February.

Everything changes when a dead man walks into his office.

Not literally — Marco Bellini is very much alive, just legally declared dead by the Bellini crime family who want him to stay that way. Marco faked his death to escape the family business, burned his own funeral home as a cover, and accidentally took the wrong briefcase — one containing the ledger of every dirty deal the Bellinis have run in the last decade. Now every hitman in Breck is looking for him, and in a moment of spectacular bad judgment, Marco has decided that Ashford Finch is the man to protect him.

Ashford does not want this case. Ashford wants a sandwich and a nap.

Together, they are catastrophically unqualified for what follows. Ashford investigates crime scenes by reading them completely wrong and somehow arriving at the correct conclusion. Marco, who spent fifteen years running funeral logistics for the mob, keeps accidentally treating every dangerous situation like a catering emergency — calmly arranging chairs while men with guns argue around him. Their partnership makes no tactical sense, offends every professional in law enforcement, and is somehow the most effective thing either of them has ever done.

Chasing them: the Bellini family's head enforcer, a woman named Sera who finds the whole situation personally insulting. Following them: Detective Yolanda Park, who has been trying to bring down the Bellinis for four years and cannot decide if Ashford is an asset or a liability. Ahead of them: a conspiracy that goes higher than the Bellinis, deeper than the ledger, and somehow involves the city's most beloved bakery chain.

The case is real. The danger is real. The detective is not, by any reasonable standard, real — but he is the only one they have.
